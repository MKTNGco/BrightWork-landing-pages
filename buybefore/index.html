The Problem

You're negotiating with one hand tied behind your back.

In a competitive market, contingent offers lose. When your offer depends on selling your current home first, sellers see that as risk. They'll take the cleaner offer, even if yours is higher on paper.

So most sellers try to sell first. But selling a home while you're still living in it is harder than it looks. Showings to schedule around your life, repairs to coordinate while you're still there, a home staged around your furniture instead of a buyer's imagination. It costs time and, typically, money.

The conventional sequence forces a compromise on both sides of the transaction. You get a weaker position as a buyer and a lower net as a seller.

The Solution

Buy first. Then sell properly.

When you move before you sell, both sides of the transaction improve. You're no longer contingent on the buy side, so your offer competes on equal footing with everyone. And your current home goes to market the right way: vacant, properly prepped, staged, and shown at its best.

The result is typically a stronger purchase and a higher net on the sale. Not because of a program feature, but because you've removed the constraints that make both sides harder.

Ben has access to multiple structures that make this work. Which one makes sense depends on your specific situation, which is where the conversation starts.

Common Questions

Buy Before You Sell, Explained

Short, plain answers to the questions Lamorinda sellers ask Ben most often before they get started.

How does buy before you sell work in California?

Buy before you sell lets you purchase your next home without making the offer contingent on selling your current one. Through programs BrightWork maintains access to, a qualified seller can receive a purchase price on the current home and use that certainty to make a non-contingent offer on the next one. After you move, the current home is prepped, staged, and listed properly, then sold from a position of strength rather than under deadline pressure.

What is the difference between buy before you sell and a bridge loan?

A bridge loan adds debt against your current home so you can put a down payment on the next one, and you carry both payments until the sale closes. A buy before you sell program is structured differently. It removes the contingency on your purchase offer and gives you certainty on the current home up front, with repayment coming out of the eventual sale proceeds. Which structure makes sense depends on your equity, your timeline, and the math on both homes. Ben walks through the comparison before recommending a path.

Can I make a non-contingent offer before selling my home in Lamorinda?

Yes. That is the entire point of this approach. In a competitive Lamorinda market, contingent offers regularly lose to cleaner ones, even when the contingent number is higher. A buy before you sell structure gives you a non-contingent position on the purchase so your offer is judged on its merits, not on the risk attached to it. The structure that gets you there depends on your specific situation.

What happens if my current home sells for more than expected?

You keep the upside. The program provides certainty on a baseline purchase price for your current home, but if the actual sale comes in higher than that baseline, the additional proceeds flow to you after program costs are settled. This is one of the main reasons sellers prefer this structure to a rushed sale: you do not have to compromise on the listing strategy to chase a deadline, so the home tends to sell for what it should.

A note on program specifics: Buy-before-you-sell structures vary in eligibility, cost, and terms depending on your individual situation.
