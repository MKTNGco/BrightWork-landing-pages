The Forensic Review

We start with the review, not the agreement.

Before Ben asks you to sign anything, he does a forensic review of your previous listing. Five things he looks at:

  1. Pricing in context

    Not just whether the number was high, but whether it was aligned with the home's condition and what comparable sales, the similar homes in your neighborhood that actually sold, supported. Sometimes the price was in the right range but the presentation didn't back it up.

  2. Photography and visual storytelling

    Did the photos show someone a home they wanted to walk into, or did they just prove the home exists? Poor photography buries good homes.

  3. Listing copy and narrative

    Did the description speak to the buyers who value your home and your neighborhood? "Updated kitchen" means nothing. "Wolf range, custom cabinetry, and sightlines to the 5th fairway" means something.

  4. Portal placement and campaign execution

    Was your home featured where serious buyers actually spend their time? Zillow Showcase, which gives a listing enhanced placement on the site where most buyers start their search, makes a measurable difference. Most agents don't use it.

  5. Prep and improvement decisions

    Were you given a clear-eyed, prioritized list of what to fix? Were you given a realistic way to pay for it? There's a lot of ground between "this house needs everything" and "this house is perfect." The goal is "good enough," meaning a buyer walks in and says "I could live here" rather than immediately pricing in six months of renovations.

What Actually Changes

A relaunch isn't a relist with new photos.

After the review, here's what gets rebuilt before your home goes back in front of buyers.

  • New photography and video. A re-shoot, not a refresh.
  • Rewritten listing narrative. Specific language built for how Lamorinda buyers actually evaluate a home.
  • A pricing conversation, not just a price cut. Price alone rarely fixes a presentation or strategy problem.
  • A choice about how public to go. A quiet reintroduction to Ben's private buyer network, or straight to a full MLS, the Multiple Listing Service that feeds Zillow, Redfin, and most real estate portals, campaign.
  • Premium tools deployed properly. Zillow Showcase, 3D tours, Final Offer for competitive multi-offer situations, and access to pre-sale capital programs if improvements are worth making.

Common Questions

What sellers ask before requesting a review.

Why didn't my home sell in Lamorinda?

Homes in Lamorinda rarely fail to sell because of the market alone. The usual causes are price misaligned with condition, photography that buries the home, listing copy that doesn't speak to local buyers, weak portal placement, or prep decisions that left value on the table. Each of those is fixable. A forensic review looks at all five before recommending anything else.

How is a relaunch different from relisting with a new agent?

A relist usually means new photos, an adjusted price, and the same playbook. A relaunch starts with a forensic review of what specifically went wrong the first time, then rebuilds the strategy around that diagnosis. New photography, new narrative, a pricing conversation rather than just a price cut, and a choice about how public to go from a private buyer network reintroduction up through a full MLS campaign.

What does a forensic listing review actually include?

Five things: pricing in context against what comparable sales actually supported, photography and visual storytelling, listing copy and narrative, portal placement and campaign execution including tools like Zillow Showcase, and prep and improvement decisions. The output is a specific point of view on what happened and what to do differently, delivered before any listing agreement is on the table.

How long should I wait after an expired listing before relisting?

There is no single right answer. What matters more than the calendar is whether the next campaign is categorically different from the last one. If the same home goes back up with the same presentation, more time on the shelf rarely helps. If the photography, copy, pricing, and exposure plan are genuinely rebuilt, you can often move forward sooner than you'd expect.

Can expired listings in Moraga or Lafayette still sell for full market value?

Yes, in most cases. An expired listing in Moraga, Lafayette, or Orinda is usually a presentation and strategy problem rather than a value problem. Once the home is shown the way Lamorinda buyers actually evaluate homes, full market value is normally back on the table. Ben will tell you directly if he thinks otherwise.
