The 102 Brookfield Turnaround

The Situation

102 Brookfield had already been through the wringer.

The home was originally listed by another agent, hired on behalf of an out-of-town seller who couldn't be on-site to see what buyers were seeing. The listing photos didn't tell the real story. The home showed as dark, dated, and neglected. It sat. The listing agent asked for a price reduction. The seller agreed. It still didn't sell. A second price reduction was requested.

That's when the seller stopped and said, "Wait a minute."

The Walkthrough

The first move wasn't a pitch. It was a visit. Photos can hide a lot, and they had. Walking the property in person made it obvious why buyers weren't responding:

  • Overgrown trees on the property were blocking nearly all natural light, leaving rooms feeling more like a cave than a home
  • An unfinished garage
  • Paint and minor electrical issues that would make a home inspection look far scarier than it needed to
  • A 144-page home inspection report from the original listing so long and dense it was practically unreadable, the kind of document that scares off buyers before they've even seen the house

None of it was unfixable. It just hadn't been fixed.

The Plan

Rather than another price cut, BrightWork proposed a different move: fix the actual problems, then relaunch at a higher price.

What got done, in about three weeks:

  • Four large trees removed. This was the single biggest impact of the whole project. With the trees gone, the house went from feeling like a cave to bright and welcoming.
  • Garage completed and cleaned up.
  • Targeted paint and electrical work, aimed specifically at the items that make a home inspection read as alarming rather than routine.
  • The inspection report itself was reworked. The original 144-page report was condensed into a clear, human-readable format (roughly 35-40 pages, with a proper summary section) and turned into a punch list of repair items for a handyman to knock out before a fresh inspection. A more reasonable, more readable inspector was brought in for the re-inspection.

On the ground

What buyers actually saw

Solving the Real Obstacle: Cash Flow, Not Willpower

The tree removal was the linchpin of the whole turnaround, but the seller hesitated, not because they didn't want it done, but because of upfront cost. That's a solvable problem, not a dealbreaker.

Once the actual issue was cash flow, BrightWork helped the seller get the work funded up front so the project could move forward immediately, rather than waiting on the seller's own cash to free up. The seller repaid those costs after closing. It's a simple idea: remove the obstacle that's actually in the way; don't just talk about the obstacle.

The Result

Time to complete repairs 3 weeks
Biggest single investment $7,000 (tree removal)
Price change at relaunch +$50,000 over prior list price
Time on market after relaunch 1 day

The home that once looked like it needed a discount to move sold above its previous asking price, in a single day, once buyers could actually see what they were buying.

Why This Matters if Your Listing Has Gone Stale

Expired and stale listings rarely fail because the house isn't good enough. They fail because of a fixable gap: bad photos, a scary inspection report, cosmetic issues nobody addressed, or a listing strategy that never adjusted to what buyers were actually seeing.

The BrightWork approach to a relaunch isn't about charm or a flashier marketing package. It's direct, it's specific, and it starts with an honest walkthrough: what's actually wrong, what it will take to fix it, and whether it's worth fixing before going back on the market. Sometimes that means telling a seller something they don't want to hear. Sometimes it means finding a practical way to get repairs funded so cash flow isn't what's standing between a listing and a sale.

The goal isn't to be liked. It's to get the house sold, and 102 Brookfield is proof that a clear-eyed relaunch strategy can turn a stalled listing into a one-day sale.
