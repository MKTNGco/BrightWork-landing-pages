The Prop 13 Situation

Long-held homes have artificially low tax bases. Selling can trigger capital gains taxes your CPA should model with you before you decide. Doing nothing has its own costs. Families need to understand the tradeoffs before making any move.

The Legacy Home Question

The family home is often the largest asset in the estate and the most emotionally loaded. Families that do not plan for it end up in conflict. Ben helps families ask the real questions: Does anyone actually want to live here? Is the equity more powerful in another form?

The Options Most Families Don't Know They Have

Selling a long-held home and paying the capital gains bill is one path. It is not the only one. Any strategy involving a significant gain should be reviewed with your CPA and estate attorney before you decide.

Keep it as a rental. A home with a low Prop 13 basis and strong rental demand may generate more long-term family wealth as a held asset than as a sale. Ben helps families model both scenarios honestly, including the one that does not result in a listing.

1031 exchange. Sell the property and reinvest the proceeds into another qualifying property, deferring the capital gains tax event. You move the equity forward instead of settling the tax bill at closing.

Delaware Statutory Trust. A 1031-eligible vehicle that lets you invest those proceeds into a professionally managed real estate portfolio without taking on property management yourself. Useful when you want the tax deferral but not a new landlord role.

Step-up in basis. When a property transfers to heirs rather than selling during your lifetime, the cost basis resets to current market value, potentially eliminating the embedded gain entirely. Worth understanding before making any irreversible decision.

Common Questions

Questions Families Ask

When should a senior homeowner start thinking about a real estate transition?

The right time is before a health event, family crisis, or financial pressure forces the conversation. Families that plan one to three years ahead have more options, whether that means staying, downsizing, renting, or selling on their own terms. Starting early gives you room to involve the right advisors and make decisions without urgency.

What is Prop 13 and why does it matter if I'm thinking about selling?

California's Prop 13 limits how much your property taxes can increase while you own a home. Long-held Lamorinda homes often have assessed values far below current market value, which is a meaningful benefit while you stay. Selling triggers reassessment and may involve capital gains taxes. Understanding those implications before you decide is essential, and a transition plan should involve your CPA or financial planner, not just your agent.

What are the options for a senior homeowner who isn't sure they're ready to sell?

The main paths are staying and aging in place (possibly with safety modifications), downsizing to a smaller local home or senior community, converting the property to a rental, or selling and redeploying the equity. Ben helps families map out two or three realistic scenarios with honest tradeoffs before committing to any direction. None of those paths require a decision today.

What is a legacy home and how do families typically handle it?

A legacy home is the family property that carries emotional weight, where holidays happened and kids grew up. It is often the largest asset in the estate and the one families are least equipped to talk about objectively. Unplanned legacy homes become sources of sibling conflict, probate complications, and missed financial opportunities. The earlier families have the conversation, the more options they have.

Should I sell my Lamorinda home or keep it as a rental?

There is no universal answer. A home with a low Prop 13 tax basis, a large embedded gain, and strong rental demand may generate more long-term family wealth as a held asset than as a sale, particularly if the tax hit from selling is significant. The right answer depends on your financial situation, your estate plan, and what you actually want the property to do for your family. Ben helps families run through both scenarios honestly, including the one that doesn't result in a listing. Consult your CPA and estate attorney before acting on either path.

What is a DST and how does it help seniors avoid capital gains on a home sale?

A Delaware Statutory Trust (DST), a passive investment vehicle that qualifies as a 1031 replacement property without requiring you to manage real estate directly, allows you to sell a property, defer capital gains taxes through a 1031 exchange with guidance from your CPA and a qualified intermediary, and invest into a professionally managed real estate portfolio without taking on property management responsibilities yourself. It's a useful option for sellers who want the liquidity and tax deferral of a 1031 but don't want to acquire and manage another physical property. This is a complex strategy that requires a qualified intermediary and coordination with your financial advisor, CPA, and estate attorney. Ben can help you understand whether it's worth exploring for your situation.
