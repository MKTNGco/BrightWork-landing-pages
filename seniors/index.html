Who This Is For

Two ways to come into the conversation.

Long-time homeowners thinking quietly about the next chapter, and adult children who've noticed it might be time to start a conversation. Both are welcome. Both belong in the room.

For Homeowners

You've been in this home a long time. You're not in a hurry.

But you've probably started wondering, at least in passing, what happens to this home eventually. What the tax situation would look like. Whether your documents are in order. Whether your kids understand what you want. This workshop gives you a quiet, non-pressured space to start sorting those questions out, at your own pace.

For Adult Children

You've noticed things when you visit.

Maybe a safety issue. Maybe the sense that your parent hasn't updated their paperwork in years. Maybe the growing awareness that nobody in your family has actually talked about what happens to the house. This workshop is a way to start that conversation, not as a crisis intervention, but as a planning session. Bring them. Come together. It's easier when it's framed as learning, not as a family meeting with an agenda.

The Workshop

What the workshop covers (four areas).

Each topic is covered as planning, not a pitch. You leave with a framework, the right questions to bring to your professionals, and something concrete to act on.

Area 01

The Legacy Home: What Do You Actually Want for This Property?

We talk through the realistic options: staying and aging in place, downsizing, converting to a rental, or selling, and what each one actually involves. Not as a pitch for any particular path, but as a framework for making a decision that's yours.

Area 02

The Now vs. Later Roadmap

There are things that make sense to do now regardless of when or whether a move happens. Documents to update. Conversations to have. Maintenance to assess. A basic valuation to understand. Families leave with something concrete to act on.

Area 03

Tax, Trust, and Titling: What to Ask Your Advisors

Long-held homes often carry low property tax bases, substantial potential capital gains, and estate structures that haven't been reviewed in years. Ben isn't your attorney or CPA, and this isn't legal or financial advice. But he'll give you the right questions to bring to the professionals who are, which most families have never been given in plain language.

Area 04

Family Conversations and How to Have Them

The hardest part of most family home transitions isn't the transaction. It's the family dynamic. We talk through how to include adult children in planning conversations, how to handle disagreements, and how to make sure the homeowner's voice stays at the center of the process.

Common Questions

What Families Ask Before Registering

Short, plain answers to the questions long-time Lamorinda homeowners and their adult children ask most.

What happens at a senior real estate planning workshop in Lamorinda?

The workshop is a calm, educational conversation about what to do with a long-held family home. Ben Olsen walks through realistic options for the property, what makes sense to think about now versus later, the questions families should bring to their attorney and financial planner, and how to include adult children in the conversation without conflict. There is no sales pitch and no obligation to do anything afterward.

Is this a sales pitch or a genuine planning event?

It is a planning event. Ben hosts the workshop as an educational service and does not pitch his services to attendees. His practice is built on long-term relationships in Lamorinda, and many of his clients have known him for years before they ever needed an agent. Families that plan well make better decisions, and that is the only goal of the session.

Should I bring my adult children or come alone?

Either is welcome and both work. Many homeowners come on their own to gather information first. Many come together with adult children because the workshop is an easier way to start a hard conversation than a family meeting. If you want to attend with family members, please note the number of attendees on the registration form so we can plan the room accordingly.

What is Proposition 13 and how does it affect selling a home I have owned for decades?

Prop 13 is California's Proposition 13, which caps property tax increases for long-term homeowners. For families who have owned a Lamorinda home for many years, the assessed value for property tax purposes is often far below current market value, which is a meaningful benefit while you stay in the home. There are also rules about how that basis can transfer in certain circumstances. The workshop covers the questions to bring to your attorney or tax advisor so you understand how Prop 13 applies to your specific situation.

What is a step-up in basis and why does it matter for inherited property?

A step-up in basis is a tax concept that resets the cost basis of an inherited property to its fair market value at the time of inheritance, which can significantly reduce capital gains taxes if the home is later sold. For long-held Lamorinda homes with substantial appreciation, this can be one of the most consequential planning topics for a family. Ben covers the concept in plain language at the workshop and points families to the specific questions to ask their estate attorney and CPA.
