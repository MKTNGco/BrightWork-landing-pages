Who This Is For

Two audiences, one shared conversation

If You Own the Home

You've probably thought about what comes next, downsizing, renting, staying put, without having a clear plan. This workshop is for homeowners who want to understand their options while they still have all of them.

  • You want to understand the financial picture before any decisions get made

Workshop Agenda

What Happens in 90 Minutes

The Legacy Home Question

What role should the family home play in the next chapter, and in the estate? Ben walks through the real options: stay, downsize, rent, sell, and what each path actually costs and creates.

What to Do Now vs. What Can Wait

Not everything needs to happen at once. Ben maps out what families should address immediately (estate documents, tax planning conversations, basic safety) and what can be structured over a 12-24 month horizon.

Taxes, Trusts, and Probate, In Plain English

Ben isn't an attorney or a CPA. But he knows what questions to ask and what gets families in trouble when they don't ask them. Co-presenters from estate law and financial planning fill in the rest. Any strategy involving a significant gain should be reviewed with your CPA and estate attorney before you decide.

Trusts, titling, and probate. Outdated documents and improper titling can turn a clean transition into a probate nightmare. The workshop covers the questions to bring to your estate attorney, not the legal answers.

Keep it as a rental. A home with a low Prop 13 basis and strong rental demand may generate more long-term family wealth as a held asset than as a sale. Ben helps families model both scenarios honestly, including the one that does not result in a listing.

1031 exchange. Sell the property and reinvest the proceeds into another qualifying property, deferring the capital gains tax event. You move the equity forward instead of settling the tax bill at closing.

Delaware Statutory Trust. A 1031-eligible vehicle that lets you invest those proceeds into a professionally managed real estate portfolio without taking on property management yourself. Useful when you want the tax deferral but not a new landlord role.

Installment sale. Spread the recognition of gain over time instead of taking the full tax hit at closing. Whether it fits your situation is a question for your CPA, but many families have never heard it raised at all.

Step-up in basis. When a property transfers to heirs rather than selling during your lifetime, the cost basis resets to current market value, potentially eliminating the embedded gain entirely. Worth understanding before making any irreversible decision.

Ben knows how to bring these questions into the conversation alongside your CPA and estate attorney.

Practical Next Steps for Your Specific Situation

Every family leaves with a list of concrete actions, not just information. For some families that's "call your estate attorney." For others it's "get a planning-oriented home valuation." The workshop ends with clarity, not more questions.

Most families wait until a health event or a death forces the conversation. The families that plan ahead have more options, less friction, and better outcomes. Ben started hosting these workshops because the information that helps people plan well just isn't easy to find, and most agents aren't interested in having a conversation that doesn't end with a listing agreement.

Common Questions

Workshop Questions, Answered

What is the BrightWork senior real estate planning workshop?

A 90-minute educational session for Lamorinda homeowners and their adult children navigating senior real estate decisions. Ben Olsen leads the session alongside an estate attorney and financial planner. Topics include the legacy home decision, what to do now vs. later, tax and estate basics, and practical next steps.

Who should attend the senior real estate planning workshop?

The workshop is designed for two groups: seniors and homeowners who are thinking about their next chapter, and adult children who are helping their parents plan a transition. Both audiences leave with a clearer picture of options, tradeoffs, and what to do next.

Is the workshop free?

Yes. There is no cost to attend and no obligation to work with BrightWork or list a property.

What does the workshop cover?

The workshop covers four main topics: the legacy home decision (stay, downsize, rent, or sell), a now-vs-later planning roadmap, plain-language tax and estate context, and concrete next steps for each family's specific situation.

Does the workshop cover options for avoiding capital gains on a home sale?

Yes. One session covers strategies including 1031 exchanges, Delaware Statutory Trusts, installment sales, and the step-up in basis that applies to inherited property. The goal is to give families the vocabulary and questions to bring to their CPA and estate attorney, not to provide tax advice. Knowing the options exist is the starting point.

What is a Delaware Statutory Trust in real estate?

A Delaware Statutory Trust, or DST, is a passive real estate investment vehicle that qualifies as a 1031 exchange replacement property. It allows a seller to defer capital gains taxes by reinvesting sale proceeds without acquiring and managing another physical property. It is commonly used by sellers who want the tax deferral of a 1031 exchange but do not want the responsibility of being a landlord. DSTs require coordination with a qualified intermediary, a financial advisor, a CPA, and an estate attorney.
