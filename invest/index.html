Maybe I'm an Investor

The high-earning families who haven't called themselves investors yet.

Most of my clients who've bought investment properties don't describe themselves as investors. They genuinely didn't grow up thinking that's what they'd do.

What they did grow up doing is earning well, paying their taxes, buying a house in Lamorinda, and building a life here. And at some point, usually when their accountant pointed it out or when their oldest kid started asking about housing in the Bay Area, they started wondering whether they'd been leaving something on the table.

That's the conversation this page is here to start. Not a property pitch. A planning conversation about whether the step makes sense for your specific situation, and what it would actually look like if it did.

A Quick Self-Check

You might be a candidate if…

Your household income puts you in a high federal and state tax bracket.

You've accumulated equity in your primary home but haven't deployed it elsewhere.

You have adult or college-age kids who may want to live in the Bay Area.

You have aging parents who might need housing options closer to you.

Your accountant has suggested you need more deductions.

You've been quietly wondering whether you've been leaving something on the table.

Why local real estate

Something you can drive past and actually understand.

There are plenty of ways to invest. Most of them are abstractions. A rental property in Moraga is something you can drive past, walk through, and understand in a way you can't understand a derivatives position.

You also understand this market: which neighborhoods hold value, which school zones move demand, which floor plans in Moraga Country Club sell fastest and for what. That isn't a small edge. It's the entire reason local families do better with local properties than with portfolios they're guessing at.

A direct warning

Be careful with the out-of-state cheap-property pitch.

The pitch for a property in a market you've never visited is built on yield. The numbers look attractive on a one-page summary.

In practice, you're managing a property in a market you don't know, with tenants you've never screened, in a city whose economic fundamentals you're guessing at. Local families who try this approach rarely stick with it. Ben's view: if you don't know the street, don't buy the house.

Four Reasons It Makes Sense

What an investment property actually does for a Lamorinda family.

None of these are stand-alone arguments. The case for an investment property is the combination, evaluated against your specific situation. Each pillar below is a starting point for a conversation with Ben and your tax and financial advisors.

Pillar 01

Tax Structure

If you're paying significant income taxes each year, a properly structured rental property gives you tools your paycheck doesn't. Depreciation, a tax concept that lets you deduct a portion of the property's value annually as a paper expense even while the home is likely appreciating, can offset rental income.

Mortgage interest on an investment property is generally deductible. Maintenance, repairs, and property management fees reduce your taxable rental income. Ben talks through this at a conceptual level. For specifics on what applies in your situation, consult your tax advisor.

Pillar 02

Long-Term Wealth

Monthly cash flow is often flat or slightly negative in the early years of a Lamorinda rental. Ben is direct about this. He doesn't pretend it pencils as a pure income play from day one.

The argument is in the combination: principal pay-down building equity, appreciation over time, depreciation reducing the tax bill, and eventually stronger rental income. Over a five-to-ten year horizon the picture is meaningfully different from month one. He frames this as a long-horizon strategy, not a short-term trade. Past results in any market don't predict future ones. Talk to your financial advisor about what fits your timeline.

Pillar 03

Housing Flexibility for Your Family

A local rental isn't just an investment vehicle. It's a housing option. An adult child who wants to stay in the Bay Area can live there. Aging parents who need to be closer can live there.

A stock portfolio doesn't give you any of those options. A local rental property does. For families with kids approaching college age or parents approaching the age of needing to be closer, this pillar alone often reframes the whole conversation.

Pillar 04

A Generational Strategy

Aim for one investment property per child if your financial situation supports it. The property can be sold, gifted, exchanged through a 1031, an IRS provision that lets you defer capital gains taxes when you sell one investment property and reinvest the proceeds into another qualifying property, or passed on.

It creates real housing optionality for the next generation in a market that is structurally difficult to enter. Estate, gifting, and exchange mechanics are specific. Consult your tax advisor and an estate attorney on how they'd apply to your family.

A Common Objection

You don't have to be the landlord type.

The version of owning a rental property most people imagine involves being personally on call for everything. That's a choice, not a requirement.

Professionally managed rentals, where a property management company handles tenant placement, maintenance, and rent collection, are the standard for investors who have professional lives. You don't have to be the person taking calls at midnight about a leaking dishwasher. Ben works with property managers he trusts in this market and can introduce you when the timing is right.

Common Questions

Real Estate Investing in Lamorinda

Plain-language answers to the questions families ask before the planning conversation begins. None of this is financial or tax advice. Confirm specifics with your CPA and financial advisor.

Is Lamorinda a good place to invest in real estate?

Lamorinda is a market most local families already understand from living here, which is its own form of edge. School demand, neighborhood character, and floor plan preferences are familiar inputs. The trade-off is that monthly cash flow on a Lamorinda rental is often flat or slightly negative in the early years. The argument for investing here is a longer-horizon combination of principal pay-down, appreciation, tax structure, and family housing flexibility, not a short-term income play. Whether it makes sense for a specific family depends on their financial picture and goals.

What is depreciation and how does it help real estate investors?

Depreciation is a tax concept that lets you deduct a portion of the property's value each year as a paper expense, even while the home is likely appreciating in actual value. For an investment property, that paper expense can offset rental income for tax purposes. The specifics, including how much depreciation applies to a given property and how it interacts with the rest of your tax situation, are an accountant and CPA conversation. Ben Olsen is a REALTOR and can frame the concept, but you should consult your tax advisor before relying on it.

What is a 1031 exchange in California?

A 1031 exchange is an IRS provision that lets you defer capital gains taxes when you sell one investment property and reinvest the proceeds into another qualifying property. It is a tool that long-term real estate investors use to keep equity working without triggering a tax event at each transition. The rules and timelines are specific and there are mistakes that can disqualify an exchange. Talk to your CPA and a qualified intermediary before structuring one. Ben can help with the real estate side of identifying a replacement property.

Do I have to manage a rental property myself if I buy in Lamorinda?

No. Professionally managed rentals, where a property management company handles tenant placement, maintenance, and rent collection, are the standard for investors with professional lives. You do not have to be the person taking calls at midnight about a leaking dishwasher. Ben works with property managers he trusts in this market and can introduce you when the timing is right.

How much equity do I need to buy an investment property in the East Bay?

There is no single number. Investment property loans typically require a larger down payment than a primary residence, and lender requirements vary. Many local families fund a first investment property using equity already accumulated in their primary home, through a refinance or a second loan, rather than fresh savings. The right structure depends on your overall financial picture and your tax situation, which is why the first conversation is a planning conversation, not a property search. Consult your lender and your financial advisor on what fits your situation.
